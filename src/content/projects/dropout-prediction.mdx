## Overview

Online courses suffer from notoriously high dropout rates — often exceeding 90% for MOOCs. Early identification of at-risk students enables timely intervention by instructors and platforms. This project builds a classification pipeline to predict whether a student will drop out of an online course based on their early engagement patterns.

## Approach

### Dataset & Features

Student engagement data was extracted from an online course platform, including:

- Login frequency and session duration patterns
- Assignment completion rates and submission timing
- Quiz performance and attempt counts
- Forum participation (posts, replies, views)

Features were engineered to capture both activity level and behavioral trends over the early weeks of the course.

### Classifiers Evaluated

Three classifier families were implemented and compared:

| Model | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| **Random Forest** | Ensemble of decision trees; robust to noisy features, handles class imbalance well |
| **Naive Bayes** | Fast probabilistic baseline; surprisingly effective on sparse engagement data |
| **Neural Network** | Multi-layer perceptron; captures non-linear interaction effects |

### Evaluation

Models were evaluated using stratified k-fold cross-validation to ensure reliable performance estimates despite class imbalance between dropout and completion.

## Results

- **Best accuracy: 92%** achieved with the Random Forest classifier
- Random Forest outperformed both the probabilistic baseline and the neural network, likely due to the relatively small dataset size where ensembles generalize better than deep models
