## Overview

Autonomous non-holonomic wheeled robots operating in unstructured environments often need to park safely mid-navigation — whether to avoid obstacles, yield to dynamic agents, or wait for a safe passage. This project develops a planner that can reach any goal position and orientation safely while identifying optimal parking locations in surrounding environments.

## Technical Approach

### Motion Primitives
Motion primitives were derived from the robot's kinematic model. These primitives form the edges of a lattice graph, respecting the non-holonomic constraints of wheeled robots (i.e., the robot cannot move sideways).

### Lattice-Based Planner
A lattice-based path planner was implemented in **C++** to search over the motion primitive graph and generate collision-free paths to target parking configurations. The planner explicitly handles:

- Unstructured environments with irregular obstacle layouts
- Adversarial situations where the robot must find safe stopping positions
- Goal poses specified as a full 3-DOF state (x, y, heading)

### Real-Time Optimization
The planner was optimized and deployed on RC trucks for real-time application, finding the best parking spot **within 1 second**.

## Results

- Demonstrated successful parking in both simulation and hardware experiments on mobile ground robots
- Real-time performance achieved on embedded hardware with tight compute budgets
